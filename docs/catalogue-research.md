# Catalogue research notes

The 150-ingredient edition contains 615 original, manually authored culinary ideas. The suggestions have not been validated in sensory trials. Novelty is an editorial rating, and a shared annotated compound is only a reason to explore a pairing, not a prediction of compatibility.

134 ingredient profiles have selected compound annotations. Sixteen leave the chemical list empty and say so in the interface. An empty list means unannotated, not chemically uninteresting. A contrast label also does not prove that the ingredients lack shared compounds. Concentrations, thresholds, food matrices, variety and preparation are not modelled in the ranking.

No research database was bulk imported. The following primary studies and reference records were consulted for selected annotations and to check the effect of preparation. They are not evidence that every proposed dish will work, or a row-level validation of the entire catalogue.

- [Banana cultivars at different ripening stages](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6222428/): ester profiles vary with ripeness and cultivar.
- [Key aroma-active compounds in lychee](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29567218/): floral and other odorants in the studied cultivar.
- [Blackcurrant cultivar volatile profiles](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6221373/): variation by cultivar and growing conditions.
- [Raw and cooked Brassica vegetables](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6385023/): cooking changes the volatile profile.
- [Twelve Brassicaceae vegetables](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6316591/): species differ in green-leaf and sulfur compounds.
- [Fresh culinary herb aroma profiles](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7996559/): leaf profiles and aroma changes during handling.
- [Spice volatile fingerprinting](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9570555/): measured differences between selected spices.
- [Processed walnut volatiles](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7270386/): roasting changes the mix of volatile compounds.
- [Roasted sweet potato](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8620389/): roasting and formation of sweet, toasted aromas.
- [Beef and chicken broth odorants](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9570687/): cooked-meat aroma includes sulfur-containing odorants.
- [Soy sauce flavour during storage](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10378946/): furanones and other fermentation-related volatiles.
- [Maple sap and syrup chemistry](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7444596/): processing and seasonal chemistry.
- [NIST: dill ether](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?ID=C74410109): molecular formula reference.

All ingredient photographs were generated for this app. The plant filter applies to the two named ingredients, excluding dairy, eggs, meat, seafood and honey; the preparation suggestions may mention additional animal ingredients.
